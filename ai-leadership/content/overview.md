# AI Leadership

Jeremy Ehly approaches generative AI as both a practical design capability and a question of human agency. His work asks how emerging systems can expand experimentation without displacing authorship, judgment, ethical responsibility, or the productive difficulty through which designers learn.

He argues that AI literacy is becoming part of design literacy, but that integration should be deliberate, especially in foundational courses. Students need time to observe, draw, work with materials, tolerate uncertainty, and develop judgment before automated tools are allowed to bypass those experiences. The educational goal is practical fluency joined to the ability to decide when a project or exercise is stronger without AI.

He created CU Boulder's first generative AI design course, *Generative Design with AI*, bringing students from architecture, environmental product design, landscape architecture, and related disciplines into a shared investigation of text, image, geometry, fabrication, video, and AI-assisted coding. The course foregrounds taste, bias, labor, environmental cost, intellectual property, and the decisions designers should retain for themselves.
