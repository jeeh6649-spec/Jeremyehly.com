# Instructor Reflection

*Working draft based on the Fall 2025 syllabus. This piece will be revised after student evidence is curated and a reflective interview is completed.*

I teach this course because design education cannot focus only on what students are capable of making. It also has to help them decide what is worth making, who benefits, who bears the consequences, and what responsibilities accompany the ability to shape other people's choices and environments.

The semester begins with definitions because the boundaries of design are neither fixed nor innocent. We look at how modernist ideas established enduring standards of function, restraint, expression, and beauty, but we also examine what those standards leave out. Non-Western and Indigenous perspectives create an essential interruption, allowing students to see that values presented as universal often emerge from particular histories and cultures.

The final portion of the course turns toward professional responsibility. Environmental impact, accessibility, consumption, safety, security, artificial intelligence, and economic systems are not secondary constraints placed on an otherwise complete design. They are part of the design problem from the beginning.

My goal is not to hand students a single correct position. It is to help them form principles they can explain, test, and use when decisions become complicated. A personal code of ethics matters only if it can guide action under pressure, including the willingness to question a client, a brief, a technology, or one's own assumptions.
