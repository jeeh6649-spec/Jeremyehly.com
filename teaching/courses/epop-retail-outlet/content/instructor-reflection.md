# Instructor Reflection

*Working draft based on the syllabus and project archive. This piece will be revised after a reflective interview.*

EPOP was an experiment in giving students responsibility for an entire design ecosystem. The work was not complete when a product was fabricated or a retail proposal was presented. The class also had to determine how products would be selected, transported, displayed, priced, explained, marketed, and encountered by the public.

That breadth made the studio demanding, but it also made the connections among disciplines visible. Students learned that the quality of an object can be diminished or amplified by its identity, packaging, placement, lighting, and the larger environment in which someone encounters it. They also confronted the practical consequences of budget, lead time, structural stability, assembly, storage, and repeated use.

The course paired collective work with individual design voice. Proposal teams developed competing approaches to the outlet, while each student created products within the shared framework. Moving into construction required the class to transition from advocacy for individual ideas to responsibility for a common result.

I am especially proud of the entrepreneurial confidence the project encouraged. Students saw their work leave the studio and enter a public setting where people could handle it, respond to it, and choose to purchase it. That exchange made questions of value concrete, including the more difficult question of what products deserve to exist in a world already full of things.
