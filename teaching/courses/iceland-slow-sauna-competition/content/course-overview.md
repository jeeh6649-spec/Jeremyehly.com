# Iceland Slow Sauna Competition

**ARCH 4100-11 | Capstone in Architecture | Spring 2026 | University of Colorado Boulder**

Designing less can demand more attention.

This capstone studio asked students to slow down and engage architecture through care, intentionality, and presence. The semester centered on the Iceland Slow Sauna Competition: a compact sauna and greenhouse retreat situated within the volcanic landscape of Lake Mývatn in Northern Iceland.

The limited program created space for depth. Rather than using complexity as a substitute for resolution, students treated every square meter as consequential. They developed proposals through spatial sequence, materiality, structure, detailing, environmental response, and the relationship between interior ritual and the surrounding land.

## Architecture as Lived Experience

The studio treated architecture as something felt as well as seen. Local visits to saunas, steam rooms, and wellness spaces gave students direct experience of heat, cold, light, enclosure, transition, rest, and social ritual. These observations became design evidence rather than atmosphere alone.

The resulting projects explore distinct positions: architecture that must be approached slowly; a shared hearth organized around seasonal darkness and light; a buried passage from compression to illumination; and a retreat shaped by the spatial logic of basalt lava tubes.

## A Professional Framework

Working within an international competition gave the semester a defined external brief, deadline, and audience. Students moved from research and site analysis through conceptual development, midterm review, formal refinement, detailing, visualization, a juried review, and final competition boards.

Drawings, visualizations, and layouts were treated as tools for thinking throughout the process, not simply as presentation applied at the end. The final work required students to connect a conceptual position to spatial organization and then carry that intent through materials, assemblies, atmosphere, and representation.

## Course Outcomes

By the end of the studio, students were expected to:

- Develop a complete architectural proposal in response to an external design brief.
- Synthesize research and site analysis into architectural decisions.
- Translate conceptual ideas into spatial, formal, and programmatic organization.
- Advance work through iteration, critique, and refinement.
- Integrate materiality, structure, and detailing into a cohesive proposal.
- Communicate architectural intent through drawings, visualizations, and layouts.
- Present and defend design work in professional and academic settings.
- Contribute to a critical, collaborative studio culture.

The course positioned restraint as a form of rigor. Its central question was not how much architecture students could produce, but how fully they could resolve an experience of landscape, ritual, and renewal.
