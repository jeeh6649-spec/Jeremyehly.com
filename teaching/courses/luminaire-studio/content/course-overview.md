# Luminaire Studio

**ENVD 1110-221 | Fall 2024 | Eight-week studio**

Light as material, atmosphere, and product.

Luminaire Studio introduced students to environmental product design through one demanding outcome: a fully functioning light fixture. The project combined user experience, material behavior, digital modeling, lighting fundamentals, fabrication, and visual communication within an accelerated eight-week studio.

Students used Denver Botanic Gardens as a context for observation rather than a source of literal forms. Guided by the relationship between concept, context, and content, they documented plants, landscapes, structures, materials, and moments of light. Those observations became design principles that were tested through sketches, Rhino models, paper and cardboard studies, full-scale prototypes, and final fabrication.

## A Complete Product Process

+ **Precedent + Lighting Research** | Students built vocabulary through the study of influential designers, fixture types, light quality, and material strategies.
+ **Digital Modeling** | Orthographic drawings and Rhino models developed spatial reasoning, proportion, joinery, and digital communication.
+ **Site Observation** | A visit to Denver Botanic Gardens connected design intention to a specific environmental and cultural context.
+ **Iterative Prototyping** | Low- and medium-fidelity studies tested silhouette, diffusion, shadow, assembly, and scale.
+ **Final Fabrication** | Each student produced a working luminaire within clear dimensional, material, sustainability, and light-source constraints.

The resulting fixtures are distinct in form and atmosphere, but they share evidence of a disciplined process. Each project moves from observation to abstraction and from digital representation to an object that must stand, hang, illuminate, assemble, and endure.
