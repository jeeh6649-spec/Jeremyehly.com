# Instructor Reflection

*Working draft based on the syllabus, rationale notes, and selected outcomes. This piece will be revised after a reflective interview.*

I approach the portfolio as a design project in its own right. It is not simply a container for work completed elsewhere. It asks students to identify an audience, make judgments about what matters, establish hierarchy, write clearly, sequence evidence, and create a visual system that supports the work without overwhelming it.

That process is often more revealing than students expect. A portfolio makes gaps visible. It shows where a project needs a better diagram, a more direct explanation, a cleaner image, or a more honest account of the student's contribution. It also asks students to recognize patterns across several years of work and articulate the values, interests, and capabilities that distinguish them.

The graphic-design exercises established a common language before students turned toward their own identities. Poster design was especially useful because it demanded immediate communication and disciplined editing. A viewer must understand that something matters before deciding to look longer. The same principle applies to a portfolio page, a project opening, and the portfolio as a whole.

Generative AI added a new dimension to this work. I wanted students to gain practical experience using emerging tools while maintaining authorship and accountability. AI could help them revise a paragraph, explore alternatives, or improve the presentation of existing content, but students had to be able to describe its role and judge whether the output served their intention. That transparency is essential to professional practice.

The final professional review was the most important test. Practitioners responded not only to graphic quality but to clarity, relevance, confidence, and the student's ability to discuss the work. The portfolio became a bridge between school and the next opportunity, and students left with both a finished artifact and a more precise understanding of how they wanted to be seen.
