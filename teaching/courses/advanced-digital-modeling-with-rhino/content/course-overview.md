# Advanced Digital Modeling with Rhino

**ENVD 2352, preceded by ENVD 3152 | Selected teaching, 2013-2020**

Advanced Digital Modeling introduced Rhino as part of a larger design and communication workflow. Students developed 2D and 3D modeling, photorealistic rendering with V-Ray, advanced surface modeling, parametric modeling with Grasshopper, and integration with Adobe software.

The course treated technical ability as a means rather than an end. Exercises paired new commands with a design premise, a visual narrative, or a problem worth exploring. Students were expected to experiment, learn from one another, locate new resources independently, and communicate their ideas through professional graphic presentations.

## Learning Sequence

+ **Precision + Spatial Modeling** | Students moved from interface fluency and accurate 2D drawing into three-dimensional city, building, and object models.
+ **Rendering + Workflow** | Models were developed through lighting, materials, entourage, V-Ray rendering, Make2D, and Adobe-based presentation.
+ **Advanced Surface Modeling** | Playful object exercises introduced NURBS techniques and encouraged students to place a modeled form in an unexpected context.
+ **Parametric Design** | Grasshopper, paneling tools, and custom definitions supported increasingly complex structures and controlled variation.
+ **Integrated Final Narratives** | Culminating work connected site, program, form, parametric logic, rendering, and presentation rather than isolating software skills from design thinking.

The selected archive spans several offerings of the course. The 2020 Powers of Ten work documents remote teaching during the pandemic, while the earlier parametric pods show the longer development of a design-centered approach to digital instruction.
