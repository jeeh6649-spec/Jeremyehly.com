# Museum of Analog Experience

**ARCH 4100-11 | Capstone in Architecture | Spring 2025 | University of Colorado Boulder**

What should a museum offer when attention itself has become scarce?

This capstone studio challenged students to design a Museum of Analog Experience in Denver's Golden Triangle Creative District. The museum was conceived as a sanctuary from digital distraction and a public setting for shared experience, creative exploration, multisensory engagement, and meaningful connection.

The project began with people rather than form. Through discussion, interviews, site observation, and precedent research, students investigated the psychological and social effects of excessive screen time, including anxiety, diminished empathy, isolation, and disconnection from nature. Each student developed a point of view and a specific program in response.

## Analog Ends, Hybrid Means

The course was not organized around a rejection of technology. Students worked through hands-on formal studies, drawing, physical exploration, generative AI, immersive VR, digital modeling, and advanced visualization. The objective was to use each medium intentionally while keeping human experience at the center of the architecture.

This tension became productive: digital tools helped students imagine spaces designed for making, conversation, performance, food, play, sensory awareness, and unmediated encounters. The technology supported the inquiry without becoming its subject.

## From Position to Building

Students translated research into program, site strategy, spatial sequence, structure, material systems, environmental response, and public presence. Accessibility, equity, sustainability, and stewardship were treated as design responsibilities throughout the semester.

The final proposals were communicated through plans, sections, diagrams, renderings, physical and digital studies, and animated walkthroughs. Each portfolio records the development of an argument as well as a building.

## Course Outcomes

By the end of the studio, students were expected to:

- Understand relationships among digital culture, analog experience, architecture, and well-being.
- Conduct site and user research that informs programmatic and spatial decisions.
- Work fluently across hands-on, digital, immersive, and AI-supported design methods.
- Address materiality, structure, sustainability, accessibility, equity, and environmental impact.
- Synthesize research and design into a complete architectural proposal.
- Develop compelling ways to exhibit and communicate architectural ideas.
- Present and defend work in professional and academic settings.
- Contribute actively to a collaborative studio culture.

The studio asked students to make architecture that does more than house exhibits. It asked them to design the conditions for attention, participation, and connection.
