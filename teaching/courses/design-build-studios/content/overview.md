# Design-Build Studios

**University of Colorado Boulder | Department of Environmental Design**

Built work changes the stakes of design education.

These studios move beyond hypothetical projects. Students work with communities and institutional clients to define a need, establish a program, test alternatives, present proposals, develop a shared design, manage budgets and schedules, fabricate components, and construct work that must perform for real people.

Because each project was produced collectively, the portfolio credits the complete studio team wherever the archive provides a reliable roster. Individual attribution is less important here than recognizing the shared authorship that made construction possible.

## Teaching Through Consequence

A completed drawing can conceal an unresolved decision. A completed building cannot.

Design-build makes each choice tangible. Material dimensions affect cost. Details affect assembly. Delays affect other people. A concept must survive client review, permitting, procurement, weather, fabrication tolerances, and the collective labor required to bring it into the world.

This accountability is central to the pedagogy. Students learn professional phases and technical skills, but they also learn how to communicate, distribute responsibility, adjust to constraints, and remain committed to a common outcome.

## A Collaborative Practice

Each studio operates as an interdisciplinary design-build practice. Students contribute through different strengths while remaining accountable to the whole project. Research, community engagement, client presentations, digital modeling, cost estimating, scheduling, construction documentation, fabrication, and on-site work are connected rather than taught as isolated tasks.

The instructor establishes the framework, milestones, safety standards, and expectations for quality. Students learn through direct experimentation, peer knowledge, professional collaboration, and the consequences of the decisions they make together.

## Three Public Projects

+ **Luminous Ticket Centers | Spring + Maymester 2018** | A sixteen-week design studio and three-week build produced two mobile structures that transformed an improvised winter ticketing operation into an illuminated, comfortable, and recognizable public threshold.
+ **Children's Outdoor Classroom | Spring 2019** | A semi-enclosed garden space became a flexible three-season classroom with modular furniture, storage, planting, and educational infrastructure.
+ **Children's Playscape | Spring 2017** | A community-engaged studio designed and constructed a play environment for an affordable housing community in West Denver.

Together, these projects demonstrate a form of teaching in which design is not complete when it is presented. It is complete when people can enter it, use it, test it, and make it part of their lives.
