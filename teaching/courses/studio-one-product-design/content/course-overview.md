# Studio I | Environmental Product Design

**ENVD 1010 | Selected teaching, 2021-2023**

Studio I is the first dedicated environmental product design studio in the curriculum. It introduces students to design as an iterative process of understanding people, framing needs, generating possibilities, making ideas tangible, and learning from evidence.

This selected project evidence sits within Jeremy's broader leadership of ENVD 1010. As a primary coordinator for a foundational course serving more than 200 students each fall, he has aligned faculty, mentored teaching assistants, and worked to create a coherent experience across sections. At this scale, coordination is part of the pedagogy: shared expectations, dependable support, and a sense of belonging help students take the intellectual and creative risks that studio learning requires.

The course develops fluency in concept ideation, sketching, technical drawing, model making, prototyping, user identification, design thinking, optimization, and presentation. These skills are taught as connected practices. Students do not simply render an object at the end of a project; they use drawing and prototyping to ask questions, test assumptions, communicate with users, and improve the work.

## Course Structure

+ **Personal Observation** | Early work begins with familiar routines and objects, helping students recognize how behavior, identity, and context shape design decisions.
+ **Client-Led Design** | Students move from designing for themselves to interviewing and designing for another person.
+ **User-Based Product** | The culminating assignment asks students to identify a need, conduct research, generate at least thirty concepts, prototype at increasing levels of resolution, test with users, and communicate a final proposal.
+ **Design Communication** | Sketches, technical drawings, prototypes, presentation boards, and short pitches require students to make both their process and final decisions legible.

The selected work shows students applying this process to education, neonatal care, study habits, and avalanche safety. The outcomes vary widely in form, but each project connects a defined audience to a researched need and a testable design response.
